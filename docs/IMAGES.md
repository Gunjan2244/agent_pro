# Site images: where they go, the rules, and the generation prompts

Agent websites (WEB_RENDERER.md) used to have just one image, the agent's photo. To look finished on day one (DECISIONS #78) and to sell in the "Best offers" family (DECISIONS #76), a site needs a small set of **bundled illustrations**, plus the images agents and admins upload. This doc lists every image slot, the rules every image follows, and the generation prompt for each bundled image and each offer image.

**Status (2026-11-05, DECISIONS #133):**
- **Shipped locally, not deployed:** the **bold set**, generated by the user on 2026-11-05 from the prompts below (`design/site-images/PROMPTS.md` is the ready-to-paste copy). It was converted into `web/images/` on 2026-11-05 (25 names, 2.3 MB).
  - **19 website pictures:** 3 hero scenes, all 13 plan types (including the 5 that never existed: `interest-motor`, `-home`, `-travel`, `-business`, `-accident`), `contact-talk` and the 2 share pictures.
  - **6 offer pictures:** the English versions of all 6 live offers, bundled as `offer-<key>` for the fixtures.
  - **Hindi offer pictures:** 6, in `design/site-images/source/offers-hi/` (the script ignores that folder), for when per-language offer pictures are built.
  - **The user's originals** (generator titles) are in `pictures/` at the repo root. Their mapping to slots is in the CHANGELOG entry of 2026-11-05.
- **Accepted by the user, 2026-11-05:** the two cancer pictures' badges are not the offer's. The English one says "Exclusive support" and the Hindi one "आपके लिए" ("for you"), where the offer's own badge is "New" / "नया". The user decided to use them as generated. The text rule below ("every word comes from that offer's own text") is therefore not met for that badge.
- **Rollout:** important What's new item `BoldPictures` (version 9; CLAUDE.md, DECISIONS #121).

The renderer only draws a bundled image that is listed in `web/images/manifest.json`, so a missing file just means no image, never a broken one. The masters stay in `design/` and are never copied to the site.

## Rules for every image
1. **No stock faces pretending to be real people.**
   - The agent is only ever shown in their own uploaded photo.
   - Customers and testimonials never get faces, since an invented face next to a quote is fabricated social proof.
   - The illustrations show generic, clearly illustrated people, never photo-real ones.
2. **No brands, and text only on offer images.**
   - No insurer logos, product names, building signage, LIC colours or mascots in any image.
   - **Website images** (hero scenes, plan types, `contact-talk`, share images) carry **no text, letters or numbers**. Where a template wants words on or beside a picture, it sets them in HTML, so they are translated and indexed.
   - **Offer images carry text:** the offer's public title, badge and three facts, in English and in Hindi, set in a design tool, never by the generator ("Offer images" below).
3. **One house style:** bold, hand-made print illustration (the style prefix below), so the 5 templates and all agents look like one product.
4. **The house palette, solid full colour:**
   - Every image uses **one solid full-bleed background ink**, plus two or three others, from this fixed set:
     - deep ink `#1C2433` for outlines and hair
     - paper `#F3EBDD`
     - marigold `#E3A72F`
     - terracotta `#C8553D`
     - peacock `#2E6F8E`
     - leaf `#5E8C61`
     - blush `#E8B4A0`
   - Skin tones are warm and natural.
   - No gradients, no neon, no pastel wash.
   - Pictures sit in cards, columns and the Trusted Advisor banner, never as a page background, so the agent's accent (one of 8 deep swatches) still marks the links and buttons.
   - Backgrounds rotate between images (see the prompts), so neighbouring cards differ.
4a. **No fade, no frame:** the background colour runs to every edge. No white fade-out, border or vignette.
4b. **Thumbnail-proof:** plan-type images are shown from 48 px chips (Insurance page, "Other types of insurance") and 96 px thumbnails (services, Needs Finder) up to 420 px cards. So: one clear subject, a strong silhouette and large shapes, still readable at 96 px and recognisable at 48 px.
5. **Indian context:**
   - Everyday modern Indian homes and clothing.
   - A mix of ages, regions and genders across the set.
   - No religious symbols.
   - No wealth clichés (no gold, no cash bundles, no luxury cars).
5a. **The agent is the only advisor:** the site's copy is the agent speaking in the first person ("I help families…", "Leave me a message"). An illustrated advisor would look like a second agent, so images show the **customer's side** (families, people at home, people sending a message), never an illustrated insurance agent.
6. **Composition leaves space:** the subject sits in the left or right two-thirds. The other side is a calm, flat area of the background ink. That side is the safe area for responsive crops (`object-position`) and for any HTML text a template sets over the picture.
6a. **Banner-proof (hero scenes):**
   - Trusted Advisor shows the hero scene as a full-width banner (`object-fit: cover`, 260 px tall on phones) under its dark text panel.
   - So keep heads and faces in the **middle half vertically**, so a wide crop keeps them.
7. **Formats:** generate a PNG master of at least 1600 px on the long side, saved to `design/site-images/source/<name>.png`.
   - `scripts/gen-site-images.py` writes WebP at 400 and 800 px wide (and 1200×630 for the OG images) into `web/images/`, and updates `web/images/manifest.json`.
   - **Budget:** 800 px files ≤ 90 KB, 400 px ≤ 30 KB, OG ≤ 80 KB. They were raised from 45 / 18 KB on 2026-11-05 (user's choice, DECISIONS #133) because the print grain doubles the file size, and a lower quality smears it.
   - **16:9 masters must be exactly 16:9:** the web suite checks that a plan-type picture is 800×450. Generators often give 1672×941, so trim 1 px rows off the master first.
   - Offer images are uploaded by an admin instead (16:9, ≤ 512 KB, WebP or PNG; OFFERS.md).
8. **HTML:**
   - Every bundled image has explicit `width`/`height`, `srcset` and `sizes`, and `decoding="async"`. It is lazy, except the hero scene where it is above the fold (WEB_RENDERER.md, Templates).
   - The agent's photo stays the only `fetchpriority="high"` image.
   - `alt` is a translated text (`site.image_alt_*`), or `alt=""` when the image is purely decorative.
9. **Licence:** use a generator whose terms allow commercial use. Don't name artists or studios in prompts. Keep the prompt and the tool used next to each master (`design/site-images/source/<name>.txt`).

## Making it look hand-made, not AI
Bold illustration hides the usual AI look better than soft pastels, but only if the pictures look printed by a person, not rendered. Do all five:
1. **One style reference for every image.**
   - Generate `hero-scene-1` first and get it right.
   - Then pass it as the style reference for the other 30, e.g. Midjourney `--sref <its url> --style raw --stylize 50`, or the reference-image input of whichever tool is used.
   - Keep the seed when making variants. This is what makes 31 pictures look like one illustrator's set.
2. **Pick the least perfect** of each batch of 4.
   - Reject glossy skin, symmetric matching smiles, everyone looking at the camera, and extra or fused fingers.
   - Keep hands simple or busy (holding a cup, a phone, a bag, a watering can); hide them when a pose doesn't need them.
3. **Specific Indian details beat generic ones:** steel tumblers, a pressure cooker, a ceiling fan, a tube light, plastic chairs, rangoli at the door, chappals at the threshold, a scooter, creased cotton kurtas, a calendar on the wall (blank).
4. **Post-process in an editor before saving the master:**
   - lay a scanned paper or grain texture on top (multiply, 10–20%);
   - offset one colour channel by 1–2 px (print misregistration);
   - crop slightly off-centre;
   - then save the ≥ 1600 px master.
   This takes away the AI smoothness and keeps the print texture.
5. **Never let the generator write text.**
   - Generators misspell English and break Devanagari, which is the quickest way to look AI-made.
   - Offer artwork is generated with an **empty panel**, and the text is set afterwards in Canva or Figma with the site's fonts: **Inter** for English, **Noto Sans Devanagari** for Hindi.
   - The English and Hindi versions share one artwork.

## Style prefix (put in front of every prompt)
> Hand-made editorial illustration that looks screen-printed on textured paper: bold confident deep-ink (#1C2433) outlines with natural variation in line weight, slightly uneven like brush and pen; flat solid colour fills from a limited set of inks: marigold #E3A72F, terracotta #C8553D, peacock blue #2E6F8E, leaf green #5E8C61, blush #E8B4A0, paper #F3EBDD; one solid full-bleed background colour; visible print grain, halftone dots in the shadows, slight ink misregistration (1–2 px) and uneven ink coverage like a real risograph print; flat cel shadows from one clear light source; one clear subject with a strong silhouette. Real, lived-in modern Indian everyday life with specific details; people with natural, varied faces, ages and bodies, mid-action and candid, not posing for the camera. Calm, warm, honest.

**Negative prompt (every image):**
> photorealistic, 3D render, CGI, glossy, airbrushed skin, smooth gradients, soft glow, bokeh, lens blur, HDR, hyperdetailed, 8k, plastic look, perfect symmetry, everyone smiling at camera, stock-photo pose, identical faces, extra or fused fingers, distorted hands, text, letters, numbers, words, watermark, signature, logo, brand, signage, number plates, currency notes, gold heaps, luxury car, religious symbols, flags, insurance agent, salesperson, suit and tie, hospital gore, needles, neon, pastel wash, white fade-out background, frame, border, cluttered background

### 4. Offer images (16:9, master 1600×900; uploaded by an admin per offer, OFFERS.md)
Offers are the one place an image carries text (rule 2). The admin uploads them; copies of the English versions are also bundled as `offer-<key>` for the dev fixtures and the preview's demo data (`supabase/demo/demo-offers.json`).

**Layout:**
- **Left 55%:** the scene, on its background ink.
- **Right 45%:** a flat **deep-ink `#1C2433` panel**, generated **empty**.
- **Then set the text in a design tool**, in this order, top to bottom:
  1. a marigold `#E3A72F` pill with the **badge** (deep-ink text);
  2. the **title** in Inter Bold (English) or Noto Sans Devanagari Bold (Hindi), paper `#F3EBDD`, at least 72 px tall on the master;
  3. **three facts**, each with a small marigold tick, each at most 6 words, at least 48 px, paper colour;
  4. a footer line, at least 28 px, paper at 70%: "Subject to policy terms" / "पॉलिसी की शर्तों के अधीन".
- These minimum sizes keep the facts readable on a ~360 px phone card.

**Text rules (also in OFFERS.md):**
- Every word comes from that offer's own public title, badge, summary or details, in the same language. Nothing new is claimed.
- No insurer, company or product names (DECISIONS #77; check against `catalog.publicTextRules.blockedTerms`).
- No logos, no premiums and no ranking words ("best", "No. 1").
- When uploading, the admin uses the title and the three facts as the image's alt text.
- The card still shows the title in HTML (accessibility and search), so the title appears twice. That is accepted.

**Prompt:** style prefix + the scene + "on a solid <ink> background, the subject in the left 55%; the right 45% is an empty flat deep-ink #1C2433 panel with nothing in it" + negative prompt.

## Asking agents for their photo (slot 1)
The app's photo step (UX.md) should give these tips, since it's the image that matters most:
- face and shoulders, looking at the camera, in daylight facing a window;
- a plain background;
- smart everyday clothes;
- no sunglasses, no group photos;
- no insurer uniform or logos (DECISIONS #77).

The tips are in the app: UX.md, Onboarding step 5 (Your photo). After picking, the agent adjusts the crop in the photo editor, which previews the circle, the 4:5 About me picture and the 4:3 cover the templates cut from the square (site-schema `shownAs`; UX.md, Photo editor; DECISIONS #124).

## Generating the WebP files (`scripts/gen-site-images.py`)
- **Run:** `python3 -I scripts/gen-site-images.py` from the repo root (Pillow 12; optional `--src` and `--out`). It reads only the top-level `design/site-images/source/*.png` (`spare/`, `needs-fix/` and `offers-hi/` are ignored) and rewrites `web/images/`.
- **Output:** for each master `<name>.png`: `<name>-400.webp` and `<name>-800.webp` at the master's own aspect (16:9 masters give 800×450, 4:3 give 800×600); for `og-*` masters a single `og-<name>-1200.webp`, centre-cropped to 1200×630. WebP files whose master is gone are deleted, so the folder always equals the manifest (a test checks this, and that no PNG is in `web/images/`).
- **Budgets:** each file starts at quality 82; a file over its budget is re-encoded 4 points lower (with a warning) until it fits, and the script fails below quality 40.
- **Manifest:** `web/images/manifest.json` = `{ "<name>": { "w": 800, "h": 450, "sizes": [400, 800] } }` (OG: `{ "w": 1200, "h": 630, "sizes": [1200] }`); `w`/`h` are the largest file's pixels. The renderer draws an image only when its name is listed (WEB_RENDERER.md, "Bundled images"), with `src` = the largest size and `srcset` = every size.
- **Demo offer images** are used only by the dev fixtures: a fixture offer names one in its optional `image` field (copied from `supabase/demo/demo-offers.json`), and the renderer accepts it only when it matches `^offer-[a-z0-9-]+$` and is in the manifest. Real offers use the admin-uploaded `image_url`, else the interest image.
- **Shipped (2026-11-05, the bold set plus the two pale leftovers): 2.1 MB in total** (the pale set was 640 KB).
  - 400 px files: 9–30 KB.
  - 800 px files: 24–86 KB, most at quality 82. The heaviest are the hero scenes and offer pictures at quality 74–78, e.g. `hero-scene-1-800` 86 KB.
  - Share pictures: `og-personal-1200` 74 KB, `og-offers-1200` 64 KB.
  - Phones load the 400 px files.
